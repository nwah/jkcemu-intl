The 40/80-Track Problem

JKCEMU must know whether a floppy disk drive with 40 tracks (Single Density, SD) or with 80 tracks (Double Density, DD) is to be emulated. If the floppy disk inserted in the drive has 50 or more tracks, JKCEMU assumes a double-density floppy disk. In that case, a double-density drive is also emulated.

A single-density floppy disk, on the other hand, can be operated in either a single-density or a double-density drive. It depends on the software running in the emulator which drive type must be emulated. If the software assumes a double-density drive, it will send two positioning steps to the drive to accomplish a track change on the floppy disk. In that case, JKCEMU must combine these two steps back into one (track number conversion). For this reason, when you insert a single-density floppy disk, you are asked whether a 40- or 80-track drive should be emulated. With 40 tracks, track number conversion is switched off, and with 80 tracks it is switched on.

When formatting, on the other hand, the emulator detects on its own whether the track numbers need to be converted. If, after formatting track 0, two positioning steps are sent to the drive, JKCEMU assumes a 40-track floppy disk in an 80-track drive and switches on track number conversion.
