SC2 Chess Computer

The SC2 chess computer was manufactured at VEB Mikroelektronik Erfurt. Communication between the player and the computer takes place exclusively via a keyboard and a 7-segment display, along with two LEDs for "check" and "checkmate". There is also an acoustic signal. This is quite practical, since the computer's thinking time can be rather long depending on the program level. In JKCEMU, the signal tone can only be heard if you enable sound output in the Audio/Tape window on the Speaker tab.

You can shorten the computer's thinking time by running the emulator at maximum speed.

3. Notes on Operation

After resetting (RESET or Escape), select the program level (1 to 10) with the K key. The higher the program level, the stronger the computer plays. However, it then also takes longer to think.

By default, you play white and the computer plays black, i.e. you move first. Now enter your first move (e.g. E2E4) and confirm with Q (Enter). The computer thinks and then displays its move. Now it is your turn again, and so on.

If you want to play black, press the W key after selecting the program level (display EE EE) and confirm with Q (Enter). The computer now displays its first move. Now it is your turn: enter your first move and confirm, and so on.

3.2. Special Moves

3.2.1. Castling

Castling is entered, and displayed, as the king's move, i.e. the possible moves are:
E1 G1 castle kingside as white
E1 C1 castle queenside as white
E8 g8 castle kingside as black
E8 C8 castle queenside as black


3.2.2. En Passant

Capturing en passant (in passing) is only possible between pawns, and only when a pawn advances two squares, thereby skipping over a square on which it could have been captured by another pawn. In that case, the opposing pawn can capture it by moving to the skipped-over square.

3.2.3. Pawn Promotion

When a pawn reaches the opponent's back rank, it can be promoted to any other piece. By default, it is always promoted to a queen. However, if you want to promote your pawn to a different piece, you must do so via problem entry.

3.3. Corrections

If you made a typing mistake while entering a move and have not yet confirmed the move with Q (Enter), press L and enter the move again.

If the computer does not accept the move you entered (display -- --), simply enter a valid move.

3.4. Checking the Board Position

JKCEMU offers its own chessboard display, which has practically made the chess program's own board-position check unnecessary. For the sake of completeness, however, it is explained here anyway.

Whenever it is your turn, you can invoke the board-position check to have the board position stored in the computer displayed to you. Simply press the Q (Enter) key without entering a move. With each key press, an occupied square and the piece standing on it are displayed. The left two digits of the display indicate the square, and the right two digits stand for the piece. The pieces are coded as follows:
PieceWhiteBlack
pawnE22
knightE44
bishopE66
rookE88
queenEAA
kingECC


Except after a problem entry, the board-position check is only possible after the first move has been made. To continue playing after the board-position check, you must press the L key. The display then shows 00 00, and you can now enter your move.

3.5. Problem Entry (Changing the Board Position)

The P key takes you into problem entry mode. The same key takes you out of this mode again. The display then shows four zeros, and you can continue playing.

If you want to remove a piece, enter the position of the square and press Q (Enter). The square is then set to empty.

If, on the other hand, you want to add or change a piece, enter the position of the square, followed by the code for the piece, and press Q (Enter).

The codes for the pieces differ, for the king and the queen, from those used in the board-position check (queen: 1 instead of A, king: 3 instead of C). However, you can still enter the codes A and C; 1 and 3 are then displayed instead.

The following codes therefore apply to problem entry:
PieceWhiteBlack
pawnE22
knightE44
bishopE66
rookE88
queenE11
kingE33


4. Miscellaneous

The SC2 chess computer was initially shipped with software that was not entirely free of bugs. In certain cases, the check indicator would light up even though there was no check situation at all. There were also cases where the indicator failed to light up even though check had been given. These occasional false indications are also described in the addendum to the operating manual. Later, the chess computer was shipped with corrected software. JKCEMU includes the corrected version.
